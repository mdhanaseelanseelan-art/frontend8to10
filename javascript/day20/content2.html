A REALITY CHECK FOR MODERN SOCIETY

What If We Don't Have IT? What Would the World Be Like?

A vivid, thought-provoking exploration of how our banks, hospitals, grocery stores, electricity grids, and daily life would freeze if Information Technology ceased to exist.

⏱️ 8 min read • 👤 Plain English Guide • 🔒 Member Exclusive
🤔 A Common Thought: People often say: "Why do we need so many computers, mobile apps, and IT companies? Our ancestors lived peacefully without them, why can't we?" While that sounds romantic, the reality is startling: today, 8 billion people rely on IT not for luxury, but to eat, drink clean water, stay healthy, and travel. Let's see what happens if all IT systems vanish.

1. Hour Zero: The Day the Digital World Shuts Down

Imagine waking up tomorrow morning and reaching for your phone. There is no signal. No internet. No digital clock. You walk to the nearest store to buy milk, only to find the QR code scanner is dead and the shopkeeper has no way to accept digital money.

This is not just an inconvenience—it is the beginning of a chain reaction that would paralyze every essential service that keeps society alive.

⚡ The Core Reality:

IT is no longer an optional "add-on" to human life; it is the invisible backbone supporting food distribution, clean water, medical surgery, and electricity grids across the planet.

2. Money & Banking: You Couldn't Even Buy a Cup of Tea

In our current world, physical paper cash accounts for less than 15% of all daily transactions. Nearly all trade happens through digital ledgers, cloud banking, and instant payments (UPI, Apple Pay, card swipes).

💳

ATM Machines Become Paperweights

ATM screens turn black. Your credit and debit cards become useless pieces of plastic because the central server cannot verify your balance.

🏦

Bank Branches in Chaos

Millions rush to bank branches demanding their life savings. But without computer databases, bank managers cannot even verify who owns which account.

🛒

Global Trade Halts

International cargo ships carrying fuel, wheat, and electronics cannot dock because customs databases and digital payment letters of credit are frozen.

3. Healthcare: Lives Lost in Minutes

Many people think hospitals are run purely by doctors and stethoscopes. In truth, modern medicine is deeply intertwined with Information Technology:

📋

Lost Patient Medical History

Doctors wouldn't know what allergies you have, what medicines you are taking, or your past surgical history, leading to lethal medication errors.

🚑

Ambulances Stranded Without GPS

Emergency 911/108 response centers cannot dispatch ambulances or navigate through city traffic, resulting in hours of delay for heart attack and accident victims.

🩻

Life-Support Machines Stop

ICU ventilators, dialysis systems, MRI machines, and heart monitors run on microchips and embedded software. Without IT maintenance, critical care units collapse.

4. Food Logistics: Cities Starve Within 72 Hours

How does fresh milk, vegetables, and rice arrive at your local market every morning from farms hundreds of miles away? IT logistics algorithms calculate freight routes, refrigeration temperatures, and warehouse inventory every second.

🟢 With Modern IT (Today)

  • GPS tracking ensures trucks deliver fresh goods on time.
  • Supermarkets restock items automatically before shelves empty.
  • Cold storage temperatures monitored remotely to prevent food decay.
  • Instant home deliveries of food and medicines within 15 minutes.

🔴 Without IT (The Collapse)

  • Cargo trucks stranded on highways, unable to navigate or pay for fuel.
  • Grains and fruits rot in warehouses because inventory logs are lost.
  • Major cities run out of fresh grocery supplies within 3 to 4 days.
  • Panic buying and price gouging lead to widespread civil unrest.

5. Electricity & Clean Drinking Water: The Ultimate Blackout

Modern power generation—from hydroelectric dams to nuclear and solar plants—is balanced by computer software called Smart Grids. Electricity cannot easily be stored in large quantities; generation must match consumption instantly every second.

Without IT monitoring, electrical frequencies would drift, tripping circuit breakers and throwing entire nations into total blackout. When electricity fails, city water treatment pumps stop running, and tap water supplies dry up within hours.

6. Government Services & Public Welfare: The Disadvantaged Suffer Most

In many countries, ration food distribution, pensions for senior citizens, scholarship stipends for underprivileged students, and disability subsidies are delivered using digital biometric verification. Without IT, millions of vulnerable families who depend on daily public welfare would be left stranded with zero support.

"Information Technology is not a luxury for the rich; it is the fundamental oxygen that keeps 8 billion human beings fed, safe, and connected."
